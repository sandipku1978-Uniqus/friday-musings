    Review notes

    Five things the cube tells a finance team

    1. ✓1
      Layers, in order.You can’t do step 5 before step 2. A close runs the same way: cash, subledgers, intercompany, then the top-side work. Skipping ahead feels fast and ends in a reopened period.
    2. ✓2
      Protect what’s already solved.Every recipe lifts finished work on purpose and puts it back. That’s change management: when you touch a control, re-test the ones next to it. Watch the close tracker: signed-off workstreams flip to re-testing while a recipe runs, then sign off again.
    3. ✓3
      Trust the mess in the middle.Step 7 makes the finished white side look broken, then fixes it. Every controller who has cleared a suspense account knows that week. Finish the recipe; don’t stop at the ugly part.
    4. ✓4
      A recipe beats talent.The four-turn magic trick does the work in step 2, sits inside step 4, and comes back on the bottom in step 7. A good control is a recipe a new hire can run, not a gift you hope stays with the company.
    5. ✓5
      Repeatable beats optimal.A computer can solve any position in 20 moves or fewer. People use a longer method they can repeat and explain. Optimal is for computers; repeatable is for teams, and for the auditors who have to follow them.
