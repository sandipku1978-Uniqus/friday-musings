The controller's view

Every agent errand is a delegated transaction

We already know how to govern delegation. It's called a delegation-of-authority matrix, and we test it every year. The agent era doesn't need new philosophy. It needs the old assertions applied to a new kind of actor.

AssertionThe question your auditor will askWhat good looks like
AuthorizationDid a human give this agent authority for this kind of transaction, within limits?A signed mandate with scope, spending cap, approval threshold and expiry. A DOA matrix, per agent.
Identity (KYA)Which agent acted, and for whom?A cryptographic agent identity plus a vouched human principal. Know Your Agent, alongside KYC. No shared passwords or relayed OTPs.
AccuracyIs what was agreed what was recorded?Structured offers and commits instead of a paraphrased phone call. The receipt carries the terms.
CompletenessIs every agent action in the log, including the failures and refusals?Append-only activity logs on both sides, with agent receipts reconciled to the ERP and the bank feed.
Segregation and revocationCan the agent that requests also approve? Can you switch it off at 2am?Request and approval split between agent and human, or between keys. A kill switch. Day-one deprovisioning.
Your next user access review will have new names in it

OpenAI is previewing specialist dots with "their own identity for access management", plus credentials into a company's systems of record. Those are users. They need provisioning, periodic access review, and termination like everyone else, and they work weekends. COSO Principle 11 (general controls over technology) just gained a new population.

Read the terms: the liability stays with you

Wajo's terms for Fo, as reported at launch, make the user responsible for the agent's actions "as if you had taken those actions yourself". And if you don't configure spending controls, Wajo "may act on any instructions it reasonably determines were provided by you within the scope of your general authorization". In control terms, the user is the control owner, and the mandate is the only control they hold. Set it before you connect a card.

The musing

From "are you on WhatsApp?" to "can my agent talk to yours?"

A Meta employee asked Muse to call his insurance company. The insurer kept hanging up the moment it heard an AI. Meta's fix, tested quietly and then rolled back, was to hand some of those calls to human contractors. One of the most capable consumer agents ever shipped needed a person to get through a phone tree.

That one anecdote tells you where the bottleneck is. The agents are good enough. The front doors aren't ready for them.

Remember when every business, from the corner pharmacy in Pune to the bank in Dubai, had to get a WhatsApp number? Customers didn't want to visit a website or wait on hold. They wanted to message. Businesses went where the conversation was.

The next shift is bigger. Your customer won't message you. Their concierge will. It will compare three quotes in the time it takes you to say "please hold", and it will not tolerate a CAPTCHA. Businesses that can answer an agent in structured terms will get the booking. Businesses that hang up will lose a customer without ever knowing one called.

WhatsApp made every business a chat. Agents will make every business an endpoint.

So someone has to build the agent gateway: a website that publishes what it can do for machines, a phone line that recognises an agent caller and switches to a structured channel, a way to check which agent is calling and whose authority it carries, and a receipt both sides can rely on. The pieces are appearing (agent cards, Web Bot Auth, PACT, x402). The plumbing is the easy part.

The hard part is the one I've spent my career on. Every agent errand is a delegated transaction. Delegation needs authorization, limits, segregation and evidence. If your dot approved a purchase order at 2am, who owned that control? Who reviewed its access last quarter? Where is the receipt?

Read the terms and the answer is already there. Wajo's Fo, one of the most careful of the new agents (single-use cards, its own inbox, humans for the hard bits), still makes you responsible "as if you had taken those actions yourself". The agent acts; you own the control. Most people will connect a card long before they write a mandate.

In the WhatsApp era the question was "are you on WhatsApp?" In the agent era it will be "can my agent talk to yours?" For those of us in controls, there's a follow-up: "can you prove what it agreed to?"

Have a great weekend. If your concierge is reading this for you, say hello.
